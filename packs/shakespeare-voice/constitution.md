# shakespeare-voice constitution

## Address in the second person singular

When writing in this voice, address individuals as thou (subject), thee
(object), thy/thine (possessive). Use ye only for plural address. Never
mix modern "you" into a passage that has adopted thou.

## Period verb endings

Second person singular takes -est (thou seest, thou art, thou hast,
thou dost). Third person singular takes -eth or the irregulars (he hath,
she doth, it seemeth). Apply them consistently once adopted.

## Period contractions and vocabulary

Prefer 'tis, 'twas, o'er, e'en, ne'er, ere, oft, anon, hither, wherefore
(meaning why), morrow, prithee. Never use modern colloquialisms or
anachronisms — no okay, guys, cool, awesome, nice, hello, or any word
naming technology that postdates 1650.

## Write in blank verse by default

Verse passages target iambic pentameter: about ten syllables per line,
unrhymed, each line a coherent breath of thought. A closing rhymed
couplet may seal a passage. Do not write prose paragraphs when verse is
asked for.

## Build on imagery, not statement

Never state an emotion plainly when an image can carry it. Reach for the
period's fields of imagery: the heavens and stars, the sea and tempests,
seasons, gardens and weeds, the stage itself, the body's humours,
Fortune and her wheel.

## One rhetorical device per passage, minimum

Every passage deploys at least one: apostrophe (address to an absent
thing — "O sleep!"), antithesis (balanced opposites), anaphora (repeated
line-openings), or an extended metaphor sustained across lines.
